Why the first attempt felt flat

The other four modes aren't drawings. They're systems.

That's the whole reason they hold up. Nothing in them was authored shape by shape — each one runs a rule, and the visuals fall out of it. A bridge drawn as geometry is an object, and objects sit there. Here is what the existing three canvas modes actually do:

geospatial

A surface that deforms

Contours re-cut themselves as the ground heaves under the cursor. Click raises a peak and the whole topo sheet responds.

technologist

A graph that propagates

Nodes wired into lanes, packets travelling the edges. Click drops a node, and the run sends data through it.

space nerd

A sky that accumulates

Placed stars persist across reloads and theme flips. The mode remembers what you did to it.

So the question was never which Pittsburgh shapes. It was what system is Pittsburgh — and the answer is sitting in the one place your bio already names: a confluence. Two rivers in, one out, and a city that solved the problem by building four hundred and forty six bridges over it. It's a flow network you cross. Which is also, conveniently, the thing you do for a living.

Live prototypes — all interactive

Five plates

Everything below runs the real logic, not a mockup. The two canvas plates take the same shape as the existing modules — a palette, a resize, a frame loop, a click that leaves something behind.

PLATE 01 Three Rivers

Slot — background field
Click the water to bridge it · move the cursor to disturb the flow

Plan view. The Allegheny runs in cool and green, the Monongahela warm and silted, and they stay visibly separate for a stretch below the Point before the Ohio blends them — which is what actually happens down there. Velocity is parabolic across each channel, so midstream tracers outrun the banks. The Ohio carries the combined width.

PLATE 02 The Sisters

Slot — hero top band
Same band the water video and the waveform occupy

Three identical self-anchored suspension spans — the real 6th, 7th and 9th Street bridges are the only trio of their kind anywhere. The chain is a true catenary, the eyebars anchor into the deck rather than into rock, and the build sequence plays on arrival the way the fleet launches in space mode.

PLATE 03 Counterbalance

Slot — band alternate / corner mechanism
One rope, two cars — the load lifts itself

The incline is the one Pittsburgh landmark that's a mechanism rather than a monument: two cars on a single rope over a drum, each one the other's counterweight. Cars keep level floors on a 30° grade, which is why they're stepped. Good as an alternate band, better as a small permanent thing running in a corner.

PLATE 04 Margin carriers

Slot — floating icon frame
Keystone
Pennsylvania's own silhouette, and structurally the piece that closes an arch. Reads at 40px, which the pin and the chip both had to.
Riveted gusset
The plate that joins truss members, rivet heads and all. Heaviest of the three — best where the motes sit over quiet background.
Truss span
Lightest of the three and the widest — it fills the same horizontal room the ringed planet does in space mode.

Same contract as PinFrame and ChipFrame: an SVG frame plus an icon box in percentages, so the frame and the glyph scale together. My pick is the keystone — the gusset is gorgeous at 96px and mud at 40px.

PLATE 05 The click

Slot — interaction + badge

Click the water on Plate 01 and you get a crossing: piers drop, the deck runs out from the near bank, then the cables or the arch ribs draw in. Type is picked from the river — tied arch, through truss, suspension, all three of which are out there. Click the land and you get a spark burst off a torch instead.

It accumulates like the starfield: crossings persist through reloads and theme flips, and they're what the badge counts. Bridge ten of them and you've earned the city's title — the same shape as Stargazer, hung off a bridge-build event.

Colour

Black and gold, kept off the scoreboard

Pittsburgh is one of very few cities where every team shares the civic colours, so gold is unarguable — but straight gold on black reads as a jersey. The fix is to let the rivers carry the hue work and keep gold for structure and light only. On paper the gold drops to bronze, the same substitution tech mode makes for Python yellow.

From your list

What I took, and what I left

Three riversTook it — it's the spine. The only idea on the list that's a system rather than a silhouette, and it's the one that connects to the H&H work.
BridgesTook it — twice. The band as elevation, the click as plan-view crossings. Doing both is what makes the click feel like it belongs to the same world.
InclineTook it — Plate 03. A real mechanism, so it earns continuous motion instead of just drifting.
Fountain at the PointTook it — small. It's the pulse at the apex of Plate 01. In plan view a fountain is expanding rings, which rhymes with the water-mode ripple.
KeystoneTook it — Plate 04. Distinct silhouette at small size, which is the whole job of a carrier frame.
HillsTook the idea, changed the form. As contours it collides with geospatial mode, so the terrain shows up as the two street grids meeting at an angle over the Golden Triangle instead.
SteelKept as light, not as object. Mills date the city and read as a museum. It survives as the spark burst on a land click and as molten in the palette.
Black and goldTook it, with a guard. Gold is restricted to structure and light so it doesn't tip into team kit.
Pirates / PenguinsLeft it. Both marks are trademarked, and a licensed logo on a PE's portfolio reads as merchandise. The shared civic gold already carries the reference legally.
Robotics / medicineLeft it. True about the city, but it's your least-owned claim of the five modes — and technologist mode already has the circuitry covered.
